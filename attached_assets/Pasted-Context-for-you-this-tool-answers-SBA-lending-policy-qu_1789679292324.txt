Context for you: this tool answers SBA lending policy questions against SOP 50 10 8. A test question asked two things, whether ROBS funds count toward the equity injection and who must sign the unconditional guaranty. The system retrieved one passage about ROBS eligibility, answered both questions, and displayed a VERIFIED badge next to a citation that supported neither answer. The guaranty answer was fabricated from general knowledge and used the wrong term of art. Fix the following.

Decompose compound questions before retrieval. Parse the user's question into discrete sub-questions and run a separate retrieval for each. Do not answer a sub-question from chunks retrieved for a different sub-question. In the test case the sub-questions were equity injection treatment of ROBS funds, and guarantor identification where a retirement trust holds majority ownership. Those live in different chapters and need separate retrieval.
Require per-proposition grounding. Every factual assertion in the answer must map to at least one retrieved passage that actually states it. Build the answer as proposition plus citation pairs internally, then render. If a sub-question has no supporting passage, output "No provision located in SOP 50 10 8 addressing this point" rather than answering from model knowledge. Never let a proposition ship uncited.
Split the VERIFIED badge into two signals. Right now it appears to mean the quoted string exists in the source document, which is a provenance check. Users read it as meaning the citation supports the answer, which is an entailment check. Render them separately, for example "Quote located in source" and "Supports this conclusion," and allow the second to be absent or low confidence. Never display a support signal that was not independently computed.
Expand retrieval coverage for guaranty and ownership questions. Any question mentioning guaranty, guarantor, ownership percentage, trust, or plan ownership must retrieve from the guaranty requirements provisions in addition to any topical chapter. The 20 percent ownership threshold and its exceptions are the operative rules and they are not in the ROBS section.
Preserve terms of art exactly as the SOP uses them. Do not paraphrase a defined role into an adjacent one. Plan sponsor, plan participant, plan trustee, borrower, and operating company are distinct and non-interchangeable. In the test case the corporation is both borrower and plan sponsor, while the individual is participant and trustee, so calling the guarantor the plan sponsor points at the wrong party entirely. If the retrieved text uses a term, use that term verbatim.
Apply the rule to the facts in the prompt. When the user supplies dollar amounts, entity structures, or ownership percentages, restate them and compute the result. The test answer should have stated the minimum injection in dollars against the purchase price rather than describing the requirement abstractly.
Flag disqualifying or material facts present in the prompt but not asked about. The test prompt included a seller note proposed as part of the injection, which 50 10 8 treats differently than 50 10 7.1 did. The tool ignored it. Add an "Other issues identified" section that surfaces facts in the prompt implicating SOP provisions the user did not ask about, clearly separated from the direct answer.
Stamp every answer with the source version, the effective date, and the section or page number alongside the breadcrumb hierarchy. The breadcrumb is good for navigation and useless for a memo footnote. These outputs end up printed in closing files where nobody remembers which SOP was current.
Fix the full context panel. It currently repeats the pull quote and then truncates with an ellipsis, so the one element that would let a user verify the answer is the one element they cannot read. Show a meaningful window before and after the quote, expanded by default, with the quote highlighted in place.
Build a regression set before making any of these changes. Write 12 to 15 questions where you know the correct answer and the correct governing section. Include at least three compound questions, two questions the SOP does not answer, and two where the obvious topical chapter is the wrong place to look. Score each run on whether every proposition is cited, whether each citation actually supports its proposition, and whether the tool declines when no provision exists. Phantom citation rate is the metric that matters most, since a wrong answer with a verified badge is worse than no answer.

Two guardrails on the agent. Do not let it soften item 2 into a confidence score on the whole answer, because a single number lets a fabricated sentence hide behind two well-grounded ones. And do not let it add a general legal knowledge fallback to fill gaps, which is exactly the failure you are trying to remove. The value of this tool over a chat window is that it only says what the SOP says.
